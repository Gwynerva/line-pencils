%N Number of different ways n lines in the plane, no two parallel, can meet: the number of possible multisets formed by the numbers of lines passing through each intersection point.
%S 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 9, 16, 28, 53, 98, 180
%O 1,3
%C For each point where k >= 3 of the lines meet, record k; two arrangements meet in the same way if they give the same multiset {k_1, ..., k_r}. Points on exactly two lines are not recorded: their number, binomial(n,2) - Sum_{i=1..r} binomial(k_i,2), is determined by the others.
%C The empty multiset (no three lines through a point) is counted, so a(1) = a(2) = 1. Arrangements of n lines in the real projective plane give the same numbers. Dually, a(n) is the number of distinct multisets of sizes of the lines with at least three points determined by n points in the plane.
%C Notation: 3^12 means twelve 3's.
%C a(n) <= A400149(n), the number of multisets that can occur in a linear space on n points (equivalently, the multisets for which the requirement "two lines meet in at most one point" can be satisfied); equality holds for n <= 6. Both are at most A072964(n-1), the number of multisets with Sum_{i=1..r} binomial(k_i,2) <= binomial(n,2).
%C The largest number of 3's in a multiset counted by a(n) is A003035(n), the maximal number of 3-point lines in the orchard problem.
%C 335 <= a(12) <= 337: the multisets {4^5, 3^9} and {3^18} are undecided. The multisets {6, 4, 3^12} and {6, 3^14} can be realized by pseudolines but not by straight lines, so the corresponding sequence for pseudoline arrangements exceeds a(n) at n = 12.
%C Each realizable multiset is certified by an arrangement with coordinates in Q or Q(sqrt(d)), checked in exact arithmetic. Each multiset that is not realizable is excluded either by the inequalities of Melchior (1941) or of Csima and Sawyer (1993) on the number of ordinary points, or by an exhaustive search showing that no linear space with these line sizes exists or that none of them is orientable (admits an oriented matroid in the sense of Folkman and Lawrence). The certificates, programs and logs are in the linked repository.

%e For n = 5 the a(5) = 5 multisets are {} (general position, 10 intersection points), {3}, {3, 3}, {4} and {5}.
%e For n = 7 the multiset {3^7} is counted by A400149 but not here: it would be the Fano plane, in which every pair of lines meets at a point of three lines, and the Sylvester-Gallai theorem shows that this is impossible with straight lines. It is the first multiset where the two sequences differ.
%e For n = 10 the multiset {5, 3^10} is realized by a regular pentagram together with its five axes of symmetry. Every realization needs irrational coordinates; the certificate uses Q(sqrt(5)).
