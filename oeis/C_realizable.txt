%N Number of different ways n lines in the plane, no two parallel, can meet: the number of possible multisets formed by the numbers of lines passing through each intersection point.
%S 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 9, 16, 28, 53, 98, 180
%O 1,3
%C For each intersection point of the arrangement record the number of lines through it; a(n) is the number of different multisets that occur over all arrangements of n lines (the t-vector of the arrangement). Equivalently: arrangements of n lines in the real projective plane; or, dually, multisets of the sizes of the lines with at least three points determined by n points in the plane.
%C Points on exactly two lines may be omitted from the multiset: their number is binomial(n,2) - Sum_i binomial(k_i,2). The empty multiset (no point on three or more lines) is counted, so a(1) = a(2) = 1.
%C a(n) <= [B] (multisets realizable by a linear space) <= A072964(n-1) (multisets allowed by arithmetic).
%C The largest t such that {3^t} is realizable is A003035(n) (the orchard problem), so no closed form is to be expected.
%C 335 <= a(12) <= 337 (two multisets undecided). The multisets {6, 4, 3^12} and {6, 3^14} are realizable by pseudolines but not by lines, so the analogous sequence for pseudoline arrangements exceeds a(12).
%C Not to be confused with A005834, which contains 16, 28, 53, 98 and continues 183.
%C Every term is certified: for each realizable multiset an arrangement with coordinates in Q or Q(sqrt(d)) is given and checked in exact arithmetic; every other multiset is excluded by a theorem (Melchior 1941, Csima-Sawyer 1993) or by an exhaustive search showing that no linear space of that type is orientable.

%e For n = 5 the a(5) = 5 multisets are {} (general position, 10 points), {3}, {3, 3}, {4} and {5}.
%e For n = 7 there are 17 multisets realizable by a linear space but a(7) = 16: the Fano type {3^7} has no point on exactly two lines and is excluded by the Sylvester-Gallai theorem.
%e For n = 10 the multiset {5, 3^10} (a regular pentagram with its five axes of symmetry) is realizable, but only with irrational coordinates (sqrt(5)).
